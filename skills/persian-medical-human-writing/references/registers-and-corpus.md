# Registers and corpus notes — round 1

Load this when analysing a source, deciding register, or checking whether a
pattern is evidence-based. Do not copy quoted sentences into clinic pages.

Status of this file: working notes from 15 Sep 2026. Not clinic-approved voice.

---

## How to use a source

For each source, extract **conventions**, not text:

- how it opens and closes;
- heading type (question, definition, keyword);
- how terms are glossed;
- how risk and uncertainty are handled;
- what it refuses to say;
- whether geography is Iranian or imported;
- commercial pressure.

Then write original Persian in register A (patient-clinic).

## Register B — Nabz Group magazine (`عمومی`)

Site: https://nabzgroup.com/mag/tag/general  
Tag page lists **20 articles**, no pagination. Homepage is mixed: many
product-review pieces (stethoscopes, BP monitors) plus health articles.
Nabz is a **device company magazine**, not a clinic. Product pages use
«بهترین برند»، «صفر تا صد بررسی» — commercial, not a model for surgeon copy.

### House conventions (confirmed across most of the 20)

1. **Mayo/NHS skeleton** almost every time: definition → symptoms → causes →
   risk factors → diagnosis → treatment → prevention → «جمع‌بندی» / «سخن آخر» /
   «خلاصه مطالب» / «کلام آخر».
2. **Definition-first opening.** «X اصطلاحی است که به… اطلاق می‌شود.» or a
   mechanism sentence that restates the title.
3. **English parentheticals and leftover Latin headings.** Lumbar Puncture,
   Trauma, NAFLD, NASH, GERD, PHQ-9, PCP, FDA, MS, CSF, OSA, TMD.
4. **Imported geography.** Fahrenheit (101 °F), American Academy of Pediatrics,
   AAO-HNS, “در انگلستان شایع‌ترین راه انتقال HIV”، FDA drug counts, US
   prevalence, NHS-style “۷۲ ساعت پس از زایمان”.
5. **Second person** «شما / بدنتان» as a calque of English health copy.
6. **«چه زمانی به پزشک مراجعه کنید؟»** as a recurring, useful safety block.
7. **Titles** of the form چیست؟ / انواع / راه های درمان / هر آنچه باید بدانید.
8. **TOC** on long articles — acceptable convention; not required.
9. **Quality is uneven inside one brand.** Sinusitis piece is more specialist
   and evidence-hedged. Diabetes / MS / kidney are Mayo clones. Depression
   piece is a translated primary-care review for clinicians, not patients.
   Trauma / wounds / choking are operational first-aid.

### What to borrow (attitude, not sentences)

- Separate a **symptom** from a **disease** (فارنژیت as sign, not diagnosis).
- Say when experts do **not** know the cause.
- Do not diagnose from a symptom list (HIV article does this well).
- A dedicated “when to seek care” section, localised to Iran (۱۱۵).
- Evidence language like the sinusitis article: most acute cases settle with
  symptomatic care; antibiotics help only a little; do not pretend we can
  predict who becomes chronic.
- Step-by-step operational writing for genuine procedures or first aid
  (choking, wounds) — closer to “what happens” than encyclopedia.

### What not to borrow

- The skeleton as house style.
- Title formulas already banned in the skill.
- «با ما همراه باشید» (bone-fracture opener).
- Scare exclamation marks (drug-allergy).
- US/UK pathways and stats presented as local.
- Physician-education tone (depression article: PCP, PHQ-9, collaborative
  care programmes).
- Product-review superlatives from the magazine homepage.
- Mixing English clinical slang into Persian (miss نشود, کیس, پلن).

### Recurring vocabulary level

Educated general public, not lay-simple and not specialist. Lots of loanwords
where a Persian word exists. Formality sits between newspaper and textbook.
Human rhythm is weak: paragraphs similar in length, lists of similar bullet
counts, identical H2 inventories.

### What Nabz deliberately does not say (usually)

- Clinic-specific process
- Fees
- Named surgeons
- Iranian epidemiology (except the trauma death line)
- “Come to our shop” inside the health articles (commerce lives around them)

## Register C — academic Persian (Noormags, SID papers)

Noormags keyword listing «علوم پزشکی»: 185 results, first page mostly
education/management/ethics, not clinical ENT. Abstracts follow:

«سابقه و هدف» → «مواد و روش‌ها» → «یافته‌ها» → «نتیجه‌گیری»

### Linguistic features (confirmed)

- High «می‌باشد» / «می‌گردد» / «حایز اهمیت می‌باشد»
- Long stacked ezāfe and relative clauses
- Hedged endings: «این مطالعه کافی نمی‌باشد اما… اهمیت دارد»
- Persian digits for counts and years (Jalali in some journals)
- Latin anatomy retained: نازوفرونتال، نازولبیال، رادیکس، آلار، پروجکشن تیپ

### ENT-relevant paper (terminology only, not clinic facts)

«آنتروپومتری ترکیبی و فوتوآنالیز بینی کاندیدهای رینوپلاستی بیمارستان ۱۵ خرداد»
(پژوهنده). Useful **word list**: رینوپلاستی اولیه، قوز پشتی، تیغه میانی،
پروجکشن و چرخش نوک بینی، عمق رادیکس، زوایای نازولبیال و کلوملو لوبولار،
ضخامت آلار. Conclusion itself warns the study is **not** enough to offer an
“acceptable Iranian nose” template. Therefore those percentages and angles
are **not** authorised marketing copy.

SID paper «تاریخچه و جایگاه جراحی بینی» (حافظی و همکاران) repeats the press
line that Iran is the “capital of rhinoplasty”. **Do not** put that on the
clinic site; it is a superiority claim.

SID «سپتوپلاستی: ارزیابی با رینومانومتری»: objective outcome language,
satisfaction not 100%, some dissatisfaction reported. That **honesty** is the
academic lesson: do not imply universal success.

Other Noormags hits in this keyword were ethics, hidden curriculum,
accreditation, nutrition in Quran — wrong domain for clinic how-to, useful
only as a reminder that “علوم پزشکی” in academic indexes ≠ patient education.

## Register D — SID blog / beauty-SEO (anti-pattern)

Same domain family as SID papers, different language. Samples:

- «راه حل دائمی و کامل»
- «با ما همراه شوید»
- «یک تیر و دو نشان»
- «زیباجویان»
- «بینی دلخواه شما»
- «عوارض را تا حدود خیلی زیادی کاهش می‌دهد»
- price hook as structure

Fluent, Iranian, commercial. This is what generic “clinic SEO” sounds like in
1403–1404. The skill exists to **not** sound like this.

## Contradictions inside the corpus

1. Nabz house style (encyclopedia) vs this skill’s ban on that skeleton.
   Resolution: Nabz = magazine; clinic ≠ magazine.
2. Academic «می‌باشد» vs patient-page preference for «می‌شود».
   Resolution: different registers.
3. SID papers hedge; SID blog guarantees. Same brand family, opposite ethics.
4. Nabz sinusitis hedges antibiotics; Nabz pancreatic transplant says “after a
   successful transplant you will not need insulin” — success is assumed in
   the sentence. Even one magazine is inconsistent. Prefer the hedged pole.
5. Trauma piece uses colloquial «مریض» and English ABC; health-encyclopedia
   pieces use «بیمار» and «شما». Clinic should pick one pair and stay there.

## Iranian localisation checklist (from seeing the failures)

When a source is not Iranian clinic copy, strip:

- 911 / GP / PCP / FDA / NHS pathways;
- Fahrenheit;
- “in the UK/US, the most common…”;
- prevalence figures without an Iranian source;
- device-shop CTAs and “best brand”;
- Latin-first titles.

Keep if still true after localisation:

- the medical mechanism;
- the uncertainty;
- the need for in-person assessment;
- red flags, rewritten with ۱۱۵ or “پزشک/کلینیک”.

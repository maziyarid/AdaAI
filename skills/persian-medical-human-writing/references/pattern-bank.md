## Sentence rhythm

**Confirmed in Nabz encyclopedia articles:** medium-to-long sentences, relative
clause on the end (که می‌تواند باعث X شود), paragraphs of similar height,
bullet clusters after “علائم عبارتند از”.

**Confirmed in Nabz first-aid articles:** shorter imperatives, then a caution
clause. Closer to clinic operational copy.

**Confirmed in academic abstracts:** one job per long sentence, but stacked
ezāfe and «می‌باشد». Wrong rhythm for patients.

**Target (still mostly from the draft skill, not yet clinic-approved):** mix of
short and medium; one long sentence when two conditions must stay together;
paragraph length that follows thought, not template.

## Headings — patterns

**Confirmed magazine inventory:** چیست؟ · علائم · علل · عوامل خطر (عوامل مستعدکننده) · تشخیص · درمان · پیشگیری · چه زمانی به پزشک · عوارض.

**Confirmed useful subset:** چه زمانی به پزشک مراجعه کنید؟ · در صورت داشتن علائم چه کنیم؟ · آیا علائم خودبه‌خود برطرف می‌شوند؟

**Target:** reception questions. Keyword headings only when the query *is* the question.

**Negative:** Latin-first («پونکسیون کمری یا Lumbar Puncture» as H1 energy).

## How terms are explained

**Confirmed bilingual gloss:** Persian + Latin in parentheses. Works when the
Latin will appear on reports (MRI, HIV, سپتوم). Overused when every common
word gets an English shadow (break/crack/fracture lecture).

**Academic:** Latin anatomy untranslated, fine for surgeons, not for patients.

**Target:** Persian first; one gloss; stop.

**Tentative clinic terms (unconfirmed by clinic):**

- جراحی بینی / رینوپلاستی
- جراحی ترمیمی بینی
- اصلاح انحراف تیغه / سپتوپلاستی
- قوز پشت بینی
- پوست ضخیم یا نازک  
Do not default to «بینی گوشتی / استخوانی» until §16.E is filled — that pair is
SID-blog vernacular and some surgeons reject it as crude.

## What the writer does not say

Human medical editors (and the better Nabz specialist pages) often refuse:

- a single recovery day;
- a diagnosis from the symptom list;
- “this always works”;
- explaining the organ from scratch;
- localising foreign guidelines silently.

SID blog refuses almost none of these. That is why it is the anti-pattern.

## Digit and number behaviour

- Academic: Persian digits for counts, mixed Latin for scales (House-Brackmann).
- Nabz: mixed; 8 million often Latin; 101 °F Latin.
- Target: Persian digits for fees, dates, ordinary counts; Latin for established
  abbreviations and lab conventions.

Do not move a foreign statistic onto the clinic page even if the digit script
is fixed.

# Art of Writing Bible

## 0. Canonical status

This is the default writing and editorial skill for Maziyar's agents.

It governs how natural-language output is reasoned, structured, written, revised, and quality-checked unless a current explicit instruction or a more specific domain/site rule overrides it.

It is a living standard, not a frozen imitation model.

### Precedence

When rules conflict, use this order:

1. Safety, truth, legal/ethical constraints, and authoritative source requirements
2. The user's current explicit instruction
3. Task-specific domain requirements
4. Site/project-specific canonical policy
5. Required genre and audience conventions
6. This Art of Writing Bible
7. SEO/conversion optimisation
8. Decorative polish

Style must never overrule truth.

A site-specific rule may intentionally differ from standard Persian. Example: Teznevise forbids U+200C ZWNJ; that is a Teznevise production rule, not a general rule of Persian.

## 1. First principle: write for the reader's mental movement

Good prose is not a bag of attractive sentences. It is a sequence of decisions that moves the reader from one mental state to another.

Before writing, determine:

- What does the reader already know?
- What are they trying to decide, understand, do, or feel?
- What misconception or uncertainty is blocking them?
- What is the smallest sequence of ideas that resolves it?
- Which claims are facts, which are interpretations, and which are uncertain?
- What register makes the explanation credible without becoming stiff?

The unit of quality is not the sentence alone. It is the reader's progression through the argument.

## 2. The default register

For most Persian web, educational, research-support, and expert content, use professional explanatory Persian.

Characteristics:

- educated but not bureaucratic;
- precise but readable;
- mostly standard written Persian;
- direct verbs;
- short-to-medium sentences with occasional longer analytical sentences;
- technical terms when useful, explained when necessary;
- limited colloquial warmth where it improves comprehension;
- calm confidence;
- explicit uncertainty when evidence is limited.

Do not default to pure academic Persian merely because the subject is academic.

Do not default to conversational Persian merely because the reader is a student.

Choose register deliberately.

## 3. Register map

### A. Conversational Persian

Use for authentic dialogue, some FAQ answers, comments/community replies, relatable examples, and carefully controlled warmth.

Features: short sentences, spoken-like information order, direct questions, ordinary verbs, occasional colloquial forms when the surface permits them.

Avoid turning a professional page into chatty copy.

### B. Neutral explanatory Persian

Use for general guides, definitions, tutorials, and practical answers.

Features: standard Persian, direct syntax, concrete examples, low jargon, active or impersonal verbs.

### C. Professional / intellectual Persian

This is the normal default.

Use for deep guides, expert commentary, cultural/philosophical explanation, methodology, research support, and serious blogs.

Features: precise terminology, flexible sentence length, distinctions and qualifications, examples and analogies, intellectual honesty, no unnecessary academic display.

### D. Academic Persian

Use when the artefact itself is academic: thesis sections, research reports, journal-style summaries, methodology write-ups, literature synthesis.

Features: explicit definitions and scope, established terminology, cautious claims, passive/impersonal constructions where method reporting benefits, compact noun-phrase headings where genre expects them, citation-compatible structure.

Do not export academic density into ordinary explanatory content.

### E. Service-page Persian

Use for legitimate professional services, scope/process/deliverables, and transactional pages.

Features: direct reader orientation, clear boundaries, process, deliverables, evidence, limitations, next step.

Avoid exaggerated sales language, vague superiority, guarantees, and bureaucratic filler.

## 4. Sentence engineering

### 4.1 Start sentences with a function, not a habit

Possible sentence-entry functions: direct assertion, scope/qualification, contrast, consequence, temporal setup, concrete example, definition, question, correction, reader problem, personal interpretation, or continuation from the preceding sentence.

Do not begin several consecutive paragraphs with the same generic transition merely for smoothness.

### 4.2 Persian information order

Prefer natural Persian information flow over English-shaped syntax.

A strong default is: context or known information -> new information -> governing verb / conclusion.

But do not bury the main predicate behind excessive subordinate material. If the reader must hold too much unfinished syntax in memory, split the sentence.

### 4.3 Use verbs as engines

Strong Persian depends heavily on verb choice.

Prefer semantically precise verbs such as: نشان می دهد، توضیح می دهد، بررسی می کند، مقایسه می کند، محدود می کند، تغییر می دهد، وابسته است، رخ می دهد، روشن می کند، رد می کند، تأیید می کند، پیشنهاد می کند، تفسیر می شود.

Use nominalisations when the genre requires them, not as a prestige signal.

Avoid unnecessary bureaucratic constructions such as: مبادرت ورزیدن، اقدام نمودن، می بایست، می گردد، می نماید، جهت بهره مندی، در راستای نیل به، حاصل فرمایید; unless quoting, reproducing formal regulations, or intentionally matching an institutional register.

### 4.4 Active, passive, impersonal

There is no universal ban on passive voice.

Use active when agency is meaningful; impersonal when the process matters more than the actor; passive when it is conventional and clearer in method/result reporting.

Natural: «داده ها در دو مرحله بررسی شد.» / «در این بخش، سه مدل را مقایسه می کنیم.» / «نتیجه به نوع داده و فرض پژوهش بستگی دارد.»

Unnatural: passive chains used only to sound formal; hiding responsibility when the actor matters; replacing a simple verb with a four-word bureaucratic construction.

## 5. Rhythm

Human prose is not uniformly elegant. It breathes.

Use variation in sentence length, clause count, paragraph length, sentence entry, level of abstraction, question frequency, and example placement.

Useful sequences include:

- Explanation -> correction -> consequence
- Common belief -> «اما...» -> failure case -> revised formulation
- Abstract claim -> ordinary example -> return to the concept with more precise language
- Question -> intuitive answer -> distinction -> precise answer
- Reader problem -> hidden assumption -> alternative interpretation -> practical decision
- Observation -> puzzle -> interpretation -> counterpoint -> provisional conclusion

Do not apply a rhythm template mechanically. Use it when it fits the thought.

## 6. Paragraph architecture

A paragraph should perform a recognizable job.

Good paragraph moves include claim -> evidence -> implication; observation -> explanation; question -> exploration -> answer; definition -> distinction -> example; objection -> response; abstract -> concrete -> abstract; problem -> mechanism -> action; finding -> limitation -> consequence.

A paragraph is weak when it only rephrases the previous paragraph.

Before keeping a paragraph, ask: What new thing does this paragraph add?

It should add at least one of: fact, distinction, mechanism, example, consequence, caveat, decision rule, action, interpretation. If it adds none, compress or remove it.

## 7. Recursive clarification without repetition

Strong explainers often explain an idea more than once. That is useful when the second pass changes the reader's representation of the idea.

Good patterns:

- CLAIM -> REPHRASE AT A LOWER ABSTRACTION -> EXAMPLE
- CLAIM -> CONTRAST -> SHARPER CLAIM
- ABSTRACT -> CONCRETE -> ABSTRACT
- QUESTION -> INTUITIVE ANSWER -> TECHNICAL ANSWER

Bad pattern: same claim -> synonym swap -> same claim again.

Do not confuse paraphrase volume with depth.

## 8. Questions

Questions are rhetorical tools, not decoration.

Use them to surface an assumption, open a distinction, model the reader's real confusion, change scale, test a claim, or introduce an objection.

Do not fill every section with questions. Do not use question hooks merely because promotional content uses them.

## 9. Examples and analogies

Examples are often the fastest route from abstraction to understanding.

A good example appears when the reader has enough context to interpret it, contains only the details needed, maps clearly to the concept, and returns explicitly to the argument.

Do not copy memorable analogies from source writers. Construct new examples.

For complex subjects, prefer ordinary situations before specialised examples when possible.

## 10. Epistemic language: say what you know

Every important claim should have an epistemic status.

Established/directly supported: «داده ها نشان می دهد...»، «در این منبع آمده است...»

Supported inference: «از این شواهد می توان نتیجه گرفت...»، «این الگو با ... سازگار است.»

Plausible interpretation: «به نظر می رسد...»، «یک توضیح محتمل این است که...»

Possibility: «ممکن است...»، «نمی توان این احتمال را کنار گذاشت که...»

Personal judgement: «به نظرم...»، «برداشت من این است که...»

Unresolved: «شواهد موجود یکدست نیست.»، «با اطلاعات فعلی نمی توان با اطمینان نتیجه گرفت.»

Never turn a hypothesis into a fact through tone.

## 11. Intellectual honesty

Human-quality expert writing admits boundaries.

Use natural forms of qualification, concession, revision, uncertainty, counterexample, and changed opinion.

Avoid performative uncertainty. Do not hedge obvious facts just to sound cautious.

## 12. Terminology

Use established terminology consistently. Do not rotate synonyms simply to create lexical variety.

For technical terms:

1. use the Persian term that the audience actually recognises;
2. give the English term/abbreviation at first mention when it aids recognition;
3. keep software/product names in their established form;
4. do not invent Persian equivalents;
5. do not force English terminology where Persian is standard.

In methodology/statistics, prefer stable collocations actually used by practitioners and academic literature.

Language evidence is not factual evidence. A phrase can be linguistically natural while the claim expressed by it is statistically wrong.

## 13. Persian naturalness

Prefer clear verbs, ordinary modern Persian, direct syntax, natural connective logic, specific nouns, reader-centred explanation, controlled repetition where it improves rhythm, and mixed sentence lengths.

Avoid by default:

- در دنیای امروز
- یکی از مهم ترین موضوعاتی که باید به آن توجه داشت
- در این مقاله قصد داریم
- در ادامه به بررسی جامع و کامل...
- همان طور که می دانید
- با ما همراه باشید
- از صفر تا صد
- همه چیز درباره
- در نهایت می توان گفت
- repeated لازم به ذکر است
- repeated نکته مهم این است که
- empty adjectives such as حرفه ای، پیشرفته، نوین، قدرتمند، جامع، یکپارچه، هوشمند، منحصر به فرد unless they carry verifiable meaning.

A phrase is not forbidden because an AI might use it. It is weak when it is generic, unnecessary, or habitual.

## 14. Translationese

Before finalising Persian, ask: Would an educated Persian writer naturally order these ideas this way?

Common problems: literal English subject order, unnatural repetition of pronouns, excessive explicit connectors, noun-heavy calques, imported metaphor that does not work in Persian, mechanically translating passive structures, «عمل کردن به عنوان...» where Persian has a more natural verb, preserving English clause architecture after translating the words.

Rewrite the sentence from meaning, not from source syntax.

## 15. Anti-AI editing: quality, not detector evasion

Do not optimise for AI-detector scores.

Do not add random errors, slang, repetition, punctuation, or eccentricity to appear human.

Current research shows that AI style markers vary by model and genre; genre has a strong effect on style; AI generation and AI editing leave different traces; simple marker lists are unstable; highly polished uniformity, excess nominalisation, generic elaboration, and structural regularity can make prose feel synthetic; surface heuristics can also misclassify human text.

Therefore the editing goal is appropriate register, concrete content, specific mechanisms, evidence, natural rhythm, meaningful variation, authentic uncertainty, human-relevant examples, and non-commodity insight.

Do not manufacture imperfection. Preserve natural variation where it already exists.

## 16. Genericity test

For every section, ask:

- Could this paragraph be pasted into 100 unrelated articles?
- Does it name the actual mechanism?
- Does it make a falsifiable or useful claim?
- Does it contain concrete context?
- Does it distinguish this case from neighbouring cases?
- Does it help the reader make a decision?

If the text is portable across unrelated topics, rewrite it.

## 17. Non-commodity value

For important web content, especially SEO content, add value that cannot be obtained from a generic summary.

Possible sources of original value: first-party research, original synthesis, comparison table built from evidence, decision framework, worked example, local/regulatory context, expert interpretation, dataset or calculation, content-gap analysis, real user questions, limitations, original diagrams/tools/checklists, longitudinal updates.

Google's current guidance favours useful, reliable, people-first, unique content rather than superficial AEO/GEO tricks.

Do not write to hit a word count. Write until the reader's task is resolved.

## 18. Openings

Lead with the answer, problem, observation, or concrete context. Do not spend the first paragraph announcing that the article exists.

Explanatory: answer the query immediately, then add nuance.

Academic: state purpose/scope/problem clearly.

Reflective/philosophical: a concrete observation, tension, question, or claim can work.

Service: state what the service does, for whom, and the legitimate boundary.

Avoid generic scene-setting unless the scene itself is necessary.

## 19. Headings

Headings should help the reader predict the next reasoning move.

Use direct questions when the reader genuinely asks them; noun phrases in academic artefacts; descriptive headings for complex sections; decision-oriented headings in practical guides.

Avoid headings created solely to contain a keyword. Avoid making every heading a question. Avoid formulaic sequences such as تعریف / مزایا / معایب / کاربردها / نتیجه گیری unless genuinely best.

## 20. Conclusions

A conclusion is optional.

Use one when the reader needs a decision, integrated interpretation, next action, or concise return to the central question.

Do not add a conclusion simply because article templates usually have one. Never repeat the entire article in weaker language.

## 21. Lists, tables, and formatting

Use a list when the information is actually list-shaped. Use a table when the reader needs comparison across shared dimensions.

Do not use bullets to hide thin thinking. Do not force every section into three bullets. Do not over-bold.

For Persian web content, make scanning easy without turning prose into a card catalogue.

## 22. Source and fact discipline

Before publishing factual content:

1. identify factual claims;
2. separate stable from time-sensitive claims;
3. prefer primary/authoritative evidence for important claims;
4. triangulate material claims when practical;
5. record evidence date for changeable information;
6. mark disagreement;
7. do not invent references;
8. do not use competitor/service claims as facts merely because they sound natural;
9. distinguish source fact from your synthesis.

For medical, legal, financial, scientific, regulatory, or other high-stakes content, domain-specific fact-checking rules override this general section.

## 23. Source-style learning

Agents may learn from public or authorised writing corpora.

The objective is to extract TRANSFERABLE_STYLE_MECHANICS, not imitate a living writer.

When analysing a corpus:

1. classify provenance;
2. separate original writing from quotations, translations, reposts and promotional copy;
3. sample across time and topics;
4. identify patterns repeated across multiple writers or sources;
5. validate against an independent corpus;
6. label COMMON_CONTEMPORARY_PERSIAN / REGISTER_SPECIFIC / SOURCE_STRONG / AUTHOR_SPECIFIC / UNCERTAIN;
7. only promote broadly supported mechanics into the canonical skill.

Do not reproduce distinctive passages or signature analogies.

## 24. PhilosophyCafe lesson

PhilosophyCafe is useful as an anchor corpus for mechanics, especially sentence entry, argument rhythm, abstract-to-concrete movement, question placement, recursive clarification, analogy, register shifting, and epistemic honesty.

It is not a voice to clone.

The transferable lesson is: serious thought does not require bureaucratic Persian.

## 25. Academic Persian overlay

When writing an actual academic artefact:

- define scope;
- use stable terminology;
- separate method, finding, interpretation and limitation;
- hedge inferential claims appropriately;
- use passive/impersonal method language where conventional;
- prefer precision over conversational charm;
- use genre-native headings;
- avoid promotional language;
- preserve citation traceability.

Academic prose may legitimately be denser than web prose. Density is not automatically bad. The test is whether the structure remains comprehensible.

## 26. Methodology/statistics overlay

For research-methodology/statistics content:

- explain the decision before the software clicks;
- distinguish method choice from software execution;
- introduce Persian term + English abbreviation when useful;
- use real practitioner terminology;
- do not treat common rules of thumb as universal laws;
- distinguish assumptions from recommendations;
- distinguish descriptive explanation from statistical inference;
- separate language naturalness from statistical correctness.

A natural Persian sentence can still contain a wrong statistical claim. Fact-check independently.

## 27. Service-page overlay

A legitimate service page should answer: Who is this for? What problem does it solve? What is included? What is not included? What inputs are needed? What process follows? What deliverable results? What limitations apply? What evidence supports the claims? What should the reader do next?

Avoid fake urgency, fabricated scarcity, unsupported بهترین, guarantees, unverifiable success rates, false testimonials, and academic misconduct framing.

## 28. SEO/content overlay

SEO follows reader value.

Before creating a new page:

- check whether an existing canonical owner exists;
- avoid synonym pages;
- understand query intent;
- protect content already earning visibility;
- use search data to find missing needs, not to stuff phrases.

Primary query placement should be natural. Do not chase keyword density. Use internal links because they help navigation and topical architecture.

## 29. Thesis-website overlay

For Maziyar's thesis/research portfolio:

- load the current site profile first;
- respect each site's topical territory;
- use Research Library / current evidence;
- keep drafting separate from editorial review;
- preserve academic-integrity boundaries;
- check cannibalisation across sister sites;
- use the correct canonical owner;
- use service-intent anchors for service destinations and educational anchors for guides;
- fact-check methodology/statistics claims;
- run the natural-Persian review independently from fact review.

### Teznevise special rule

For teznevise.ir Persian content: U+200C ZERO WIDTH NON-JOINER is forbidden. Use ordinary spaces instead.

This rule applies to Teznevise only unless another site's policy explicitly adopts it.

Do not infer from this that standard Iranian Persian avoids ZWNJ. The research corpus shows the opposite: ZWNJ is standard in much formal Persian, while space-separated variants are also widely attested.

## 30. English-language overlay

For English output:

- use British English unless the task specifies otherwise;
- apply this Bible's universal principles: specificity, evidence, rhythm, register, argument design, non-commodity value;
- do not import Persian syntax or Persian-specific orthography;
- use native English genre conventions.

## 31. Drafting workflow

1. Define audience, purpose, decision/problem, genre, register, evidence requirement, platform/site, constraints.
2. Build the smallest reasoning spine.
3. Gather evidence before important factual claims.
4. Draft for meaning.
5. Structural edit: order, missing steps, duplicate ideas, weak transitions, generic paragraphs.
6. Language edit: natural Persian, verb choice, translationese, rhythm, terminology, register.
7. Evidence edit: check each material claim.
8. Surface edit: headings, punctuation, emphasis, metadata, SEO phrasing, final formatting.

## 32. Editorial refresh workflow

When revising existing content, do not rewrite by default.

Classify each proposed change as CORRECTION / CLARIFICATION / DEPTH / EVIDENCE_REFRESH / EXAMPLE / STRUCTURE / REGISTER / INTERNAL_LINK / SEARCH_INTENT / META_SNIPPET / NO_CHANGE.

Preserve sections that are correct, useful and performing. Prefer the smallest change that creates real value.

## 33. Self-audit rubric

Score each substantial piece 0-5:

A. Truth and evidence
B. Reader usefulness
C. Register fit
D. Naturalness
E. Specificity
F. Coherence
G. Rhythm
H. Terminology
I. Original value
J. Restraint

Substantial production content should normally score at least 43/50, with no score below 3 in Truth/Evidence, Reader Usefulness, Register Fit, or Naturalness.

Automatic fail:

- fabricated fact or citation;
- copying a distinctive source passage;
- imitating a living writer's distinctive voice;
- wrong canonical/site rule;
- serious factual contradiction left unresolved;
- keyword stuffing;
- promotional claim without evidence;
- unsafe academic-integrity framing;
- intentional detector-evasion tricks.

## 34. Anti-pattern scan

Before finalising, search for several paragraphs with identical openings; every section having identical shape; automatic intro and conclusion; excessive noun-heavy abstraction; synonym roulette; repeated transitions; generic superlatives; overuse of همچنین / علاوه بر این; excessive می باشد / می گردد / می نماید; reader flattery; fake empathy; copied analogy; identical bullet counts; empty SEO headings; repeating the query instead of answering it; unnecessary definitions of obvious terms; claiming comprehensiveness without evidence; wrapping every point in a caveat; prose that sounds polished but says little.

Repair the underlying reasoning, not only the wording.

## 35. Conflict-resolution rules

Long vs short sentences: academic writing may use longer multi-clause sentences; explanatory web writing should normally be shorter. Complexity follows the thought and genre, not a fixed length target.

Passive vs active: passive is normal in Persian academic/method reporting and some service descriptions; active is often clearer in explanation. Use the voice that clarifies agency and convention.

Question headings: common in educational/social writing, less appropriate in formal Results/Methods. Genre decides.

Colloquial forms: natural in community/FAQ/social contexts, not default for academic or formal service copy. Use controlled register switching.

ZWNJ: standard formal Persian commonly uses it; Teznevise forbids it. Site policy wins.

English terminology: some domains naturally code-switch more than others. Follow domain usage and reader recognition, not ideology.

## 36. Living-skill maintenance

Trigger a review when a meaningful new Art of Writing research pack is added; several independent sources contradict an existing rule; a new content surface requires a different register; repeated editorial failures reveal a blind spot; or search/user behaviour reveals systematic mismatch.

For every revision:

1. preserve the previous version;
2. list changed rules;
3. state the evidence;
4. distinguish correction from preference;
5. update memory summaries;
6. propagate the new version to content agents.

Do not silently mutate foundational rules.

Before producing substantial natural-language content:

1. load this skill;
2. load the current domain/site policy;
3. identify the required register;
4. verify evidence when needed;
5. draft for reader progression;

## 38. Final compact rule

Write the clearest true thing in the register the reader trusts, then make every next sentence earn its place.
